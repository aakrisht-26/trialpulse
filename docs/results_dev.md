# Development results

Research demo. Not medical advice. Not for patient decision-making.

This file is generated by `uv run python -m trialpulse.eval.results_dev` from the result files of the walk-forward runs (CLAUDE.md Steps 9 and 10). Do not edit it by hand.

- **Source:** ClinicalTrials.gov registry records, through the version-history dataset `brbk/clinical_trials_history` (license CC-BY-NC-4.0), revision v2026.09.26. Data as of 2026-09-25. TrialPulse normalizes the records and derives every number here.
- **Development origins only:** 2016-01-01, 2017-01-01. The origins 2018, 2019 and 2020 are locked (Section 10) and appear nowhere in this file.
- **What is predicted:** the probability that an open interventional trial stops early (terminated or withdrawn) within 12 and 24 months of a landmark. An elevated early-stop risk is an operational statement about a registry record; it says nothing about whether a treatment works.
- **How it is scored (Section 6):** for origin T a model is trained on what the registry showed before T (ADR 0016) and scored on the landmarks of the following 12 months, against outcomes through the data cutoff. AUC, Brier score, lift at 10% and calibration use inverse probability of censoring weights by sponsor class (ADR 0017); the last column repeats the AUC with one censoring curve for all rows. Intervals are 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals over trials, 1,000 resamples.
- **Models:**
  - **M0:** Aalen-Johansen curve per sponsor class: base rates only.
  - **M1:** static LightGBM classifier at registration (landmark 0), all feature families.
  - **M2:** discrete-time logistic regression on the sponsor class and the design features.
  - **M3:** M2 plus the amendment signals.
  - **M4:** discrete-time LightGBM, all feature families.
  - **M4-DESIGN:** ablation of M4: its learner on the sponsor class and the design features.
  - **M4-AMENDMENTS:** ablation of M4: plus the amendment signals.
  - **M4-SPONSOR:** ablation of M4: plus the sponsor record and competition.

## Differences between models, with paired intervals

The interval of a model's AUC does not say whether another model is better: both are scored on the same trials. Here the trials are resampled once and both models are scored on each resample, so the interval is that of the difference (`trialpulse.eval.compare`, 1,000 resamples). The number is the mean of the difference over the development origins. A higher AUC is better and a lower Brier score is better. An interval that excludes 0 is a difference the evaluation trials can tell from none.
